# Discussion points
At the start of each discussion the goal of each party has to be defined. The higher the internal resistance of the opponent to that goal, the more discussion points has this opponent (it represents a concept of health points in context of the discussion). The discussion points should range somewhere between 5 and 30, depending on the resistance. 
So each party has discussion points that will be attacked by the opposing party. If they fall to zero at any point the discussion is lost.
It’s possible to add a limit to how many rounds can be spent arguing (eg. 10 rounds).

# Argument checks
For all checks either WIT or CHA is used as a based attribute, depending on the type of argument. The different types of arguments are discussion/haggling (**WIT**), flirting (**CHA**) and ridicule (**CHA**). On top of that, the party with the higher [[Influence]] gets [[Checks#Advantage|Advantage]]. 

# Phases in an argument round
1. **Attacker phase**
The starting party chooses an argument level to present and then rolls a check against its
[[Argument Level]]. For the first round the starting party depends on the discussion context.
2. **Defense phase**
The defending party chooses an [[Argument Level]] to counter the attacking argument.
3. **Evaluation phase**
The defending party takes the difference between the argument levels as damage (only if
the attacking level was higher). Any failed check counts as level 0. The party with the higher successful argument level is the default attacker of the next round (in case of equality it depends on their quality, if that is equal too the attacking party stays the same as for this round).
4. **Strategy phase**
- Every party that succeeded its check gets a +1 bonus for the next round.
- The looser of this round can sacrifice 1 discussion point to be the attacker of next round
instead.
- Each party can decide to give up the discussion and yield to the other
