Welcome to F.A.B.R.I.C., an open-source Pen and Paper Framework[^1]. Framework? Indeed, instead of fully fixed rules, this project aims to provide rules for creating rules, so to say. In theory this allows to play all kinds of settings - fantasy, science fiction, cyberpunk, steampunk, horror - without having to switch the rule system all the time. Basic rules stay the same, while magic, technology and fighting styles can be vastly different between various settings. 

>[!note] Not for beginners
>Just to say this somewhere: I don't explain anything about what Pen and Paper is in this project. Also if you never played any other rules as a Dungeon Master before it might be hard to start with these. Consider yourself warned.

# An overview of the rules
Every module is marked as rules or framework. The difference is that rules are explicit, hardset rules that are ready to play. Framework modules contain rules to create your own system.

**Available** 
- [[Core Rules/index|Core Rules]] (rules): basic skill checks, character stats, healing, body modifications, character creation and leveling, opponent stats
- [[Items and Influence/index|Items and Influence]] (rules): acquiring items without having to track money
- [[Combat/index|Combat]] (rules): condition oriented combat without initiative order

**In progress**
- [[Magic/index|Magic]] (framework): structured and unstructured magic with abstract, effect oriented rules

**Outlined**
- [[Skill Trees]] (framework): unique character paths for new combat actions (fighting styles or magic) or non-combat specializations

**Planned** 
- crafting (rules): create your own awesome items
- discussion (rules): use arguments to deal damage to your opponents point of view


# Long term goal
Inspired by open source software and alike, the goal of these rules is to always stay non-commercial and community driven. People could create their own fantasy settings and magic systems and share them with others. Maybe there will be different flavors of the basic framework with continuous improvements. But not only the way of distributing the rules is inspired by open source software. As you might have noticed the whole goal of F.A.B.R.I.C is to be modular and versatile - exactly how a properly programmed project would be. This thinking might help you understand some of the choices I made within the rules. 
But all these things currently are pipe dreams of an optimal outcome. For now I am happy to share my ideas and improve them with your help.


# What does F.A.B.R.I.C. stand for?
**F**ramework for **A**rcane **B**inomaldistributed **R**ules in **I**ndividual **C**ontexts

There is a full ocean of different rules for Pen and Paper out there. I've played some of them more, some less and while many of them are great rules in general, there is one thing all of them seem to share: magic only consists of a list of predefined spells. While it is fun to explore creative ways to solve a problem using these spells, they ultimately represent a rigid tool.
Meanwhile in (some) fantasy books we read about the exploration of magic, the possibilities and creativity characters encounter while they form their own path of magic. They connect to their power, they feel the magic. And they decide what to do with it.

That is how the idea of F.A.B.R.I.C was born. I wanted to create rules that allow a caster to extend their magical sense and change reality to their imagination. Instead of collecting seemingly random spells, I wanted characters to walk their own path, finding their own approach to magic and maybe creating something similar to a spell by continuous training and refinement of a certain magic deed. And it should not be bound to one specific world or even genre. It is up to you if you want to play in a classical high-magic fantasy world or in a setting where magic is rare and dangerous. Even hacking in a science fiction setting can follow the same system as magic rules and thus make programmers the coolest class to play. This is why especially the magic rules are only a framework that should be used to create your own magic system. This means more work for the Dungeon Master (DM) but gives a lot of freedom in how your setting will feel.

Of course there is no sense in having only rules for magic and nothing else. This is the reason why I created a full rule set. I tried to be as simple as possible while maintaining a comfortable realism (especially for combat) and adaptability to different genres. And because I'm a nerd it is based on Binomial distributions[^2].

You might see why the goal of creating these rules is a rather complex problem to tackle. After chewing on it for almost five years on my own and being unsatisfied with the result, some friends convinced me to put it out there and let the hivemind of nerds work on it.
